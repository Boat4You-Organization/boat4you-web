Olbia: Strategic Port and International Gateway

Olbia Costa Smeralda Airport, just 5 kilometres from the city centre and marinas, is the fastest route to a Sardinian yacht holiday. In summer, flights arrive from many European cities, and a short taxi or transfer ride takes you to the charter bases. With car rental desks and transfers on hand, the move from terminal to catamaran deck takes only a few hours, maximising your time on the water.

The port city of Olbia itself has real Sardinian charm, with an old quarter of narrow streets, local fish markets and traditional restaurants serving regional specialities. Olbia's marinas offer provisioning, fuel docks, water and secure overnight berths for catamarans. The harbour is sheltered by the long Gulf of Olbia, giving good conditions for catamaran departures and returns in most weather.

Tavolara Marine Protected Area: Nature's Masterpiece

The Tavolara – Punta Coda Cavallo Marine Protected Area, a few nautical miles east of Olbia, is one of the Mediterranean's best-preserved marine environments. It takes in Tavolara Island—a limestone massif rising 565 metres straight from the sea—and the smaller island of Molara, a landscape of breathtaking geological beauty. Cruising here offers the chance to see Mediterranean seabirds such as Audouin's gull, and peregrine falcons nest on Tavolara's upper cliffs.

Under water, the protected area reveals extensive Posidonia oceanica seagrass meadows and rocky reefs supporting diverse fish life. Snorkelling from your catamaran brings encounters with dusky grouper, amberjack and colourful wrasse in clear water, with visibility often above 20 metres. Local dive centres run guided dives in the protected area; anchoring and mooring follow the park's zoning rules, so check them before you go.

Isola Molara and Hidden Anchorages

Isola Molara, Tavolara's smaller neighbour, offers quiet anchorages for a peaceful lunch stop or a sunrise swim. Its south-eastern side gives shelter from the north-westerly Mistral, creating calm conditions that suit catamarans. Sandy patches and clear, shallow water are ideal for paddleboarding, kayaking and family swimming, and the rocks around the island hide octopus and lobster—regular sights on snorkelling trips.

Spiaggia dello Spalmatore on Tavolara's western tip is one of the best-known stops in the area, with a long sandy spit, turquoise water and a small beach restaurant. Depths of a few metres over sand give good holding for catamarans of all sizes, and the evening silhouette of Tavolara's cliffs makes for memorable photographs. Remember that parts of the protected area are closed to anchoring.

Sardinian Culinary Excellence and Regional Flavours

Sardinian cuisine is one of the Mediterranean's most distinctive regional traditions, built on fresh seafood, artisanal methods and heritage recipes. Bottarga di muggine—salt-cured and dried mullet roe—is Sardinia's best-known culinary export, shaved thin over pasta or served with fresh lemon as an antipasto. Pecorino sardo, a sharp sheep's milk cheese, accompanies regional charcuterie and melts into fregola—a toasted pasta grain similar to couscous—cooked in savoury broths.

Cannonau, a robust red wine from the mountainous interior around Nuoro, is the island's flagship wine and pairs well with Sardinian seafood. Traditional restaurants in Olbia and the surrounding coastal villages serve cassola—a hearty fish soup of local fish and shellfish in a tomato broth with white wine and herbs. Malloreddus, small hand-rolled gnocchi dressed in meat ragù or seafood sauces, show generations of culinary craftsmanship. Provisioning with local specialities can be arranged on request, so your galley reflects Sardinia's culinary traditions.

Gulf of Olbia: Diverse Coastal Exploration

Around the Gulf of Olbia the coastline ranges from pristine beaches to rocky headlands and small harbour villages. Cala Moresca, near Golfo Aranci north-east of Olbia, offers a sheltered anchorage in a granite-lined bay, with walking trails climbing to panoramic viewpoints. The village of Porto San Paolo, about 8 nautical miles south-east of Olbia opposite Tavolara, has harbourside restaurants and makes a good base for exploring the protected area.

The waters between the gulf and the Tavolara Marine Protected Area form a sheltered cruising zone, ideal for families looking for stable conditions and plenty of wildlife. Catamarans, with their shallow draft and twin-hull stability, handle the area's varied depths well, from deeper channels to shallow beach approaches where the tender can land on sandy shores. The Mistral from the north-west can blow strongly for a day or two, but typical summer days bring moderate breezes that suit crews of any experience.

Summer Weather Patterns and Optimal Cruising Conditions

Olbia's Mediterranean climate brings warm, dry summers with excellent visibility. From June to September average highs range from 26°C to 30°C, and nights rarely drop below 18°C. Summer days usually bring moderate sea breezes; when the Mistral sets in, it can blow 25 knots or more for a day or two, so plan sheltered anchorages in advance and follow the forecast.

Water temperatures in the Gulf of Olbia and around Tavolara range from about 14°C in winter to 26°C in August, inviting daily swimming, snorkelling and water sports. Catamaran Advantages for Olbia Exploration

A catamaran brings clear advantages for charters from Olbia: a spacious bridge deck, twin-hull stability that reduces motion, and a shallow draft that allows beach approaches a monohull cannot make. Modern charter catamarans have generous cabins, several heads and a large galley for cooking on board. Water and fuel tanks are usually bigger than on a monohull of similar length, so you can stay out longer between marinas.

The stable platform particularly benefits families with young children, anyone prone to seasickness and travellers who want maximum comfort. Some larger catamarans carry water makers and solar panels that reduce engine running for daily electrical needs. The wide aft platform is ideal for launching paddleboards and for evening gatherings under Mediterranean stars.

Frequently Asked Questions About Olbia Catamaran Charters

What makes Olbia's location particularly valuable for international visitors?

Olbia Costa Smeralda Airport is only 5 kilometres from the city and its marinas, so international arrivals are quick and simple. In summer many European cities have direct flights to Olbia, which makes it ideal for travellers short on time. Boat4You can help you plan transfers; check-in and the boat handover are handled by the charter base.

Are catamarans suitable for families with young children?

Yes. A catamaran's twin-hull stability greatly reduces seasickness compared with a monohull, the large deck areas are safer for children to move around, and the shallow draft allows anchoring close to sandy beaches ideal for family swimming. With a skipper on board, the crew can also keep an eye on water safety. Many families find catamarans the better choice for a holiday afloat.

What if weather prevents planned activities?

Summer weather is usually stable. A Mistral day or a rare rainy spell may change the plan, but sheltered anchorages are always within reach in the Gulf of Olbia and behind Tavolara. Books, games and a spacious saloon keep everyone comfortable while you wait for better conditions.

Can catamarans access the same anchorages as monohulls?

Catamarans draw less water (typically 1.1–1.5 metres) and can often anchor closer to the beach and in shallow bays that deeper monohulls cannot reach. Their wide beam can make very narrow passages or small harbours tighter, and marina berths for catamarans are sometimes more expensive. Pilot books and the charter base briefing will point you to the best catamaran anchorages in Sardinian waters.

Are water sports equipment and snorkeling gear included?

It depends on the boat. Snorkelling gear is often on board, while paddleboards, kayaks and other water toys can usually be added as extras for a fee. Each boat's listing shows exactly what is included in the price and what can be booked in addition.

Cala Moresca and Coastal Village Exploration

Cala Moresca, a secluded bay near Golfo Aranci, shows dramatic granite formations rising from turquoise water. The anchorage is protected by the surrounding headland and gives access to woodland trails that climb to viewpoints over the whole gulf. In the late afternoon the granite glows golden, perfect for photography.

Trails through Mediterranean pine and fragrant myrtle scrub connect the beach to the viewpoints above, and the local flora includes rare endemic plants. An afternoon walk fits easily before sailing on to another anchorage or harbour for the night, combining sea and land in a single day of your catamaran holiday.

Use the tender to land on the sandy beach, plan the walk with a clear time to return to the boat, and you have a balanced day that suits every member of the crew.

Marina Services and Technical Support

Olbia's marinas support catamaran charters with maintenance, provisioning and technical services. Mechanics and other technicians are available in the area if a problem needs fixing, and fuel docks and water points make refuelling straightforward.

Mobile phone coverage is good throughout the Gulf of Olbia, so staying in touch with home or work is easy if you need to. On board, batteries, refrigeration and electronics run from the boat's electrical system, charged by the engines, shore power and, on some boats, solar panels.

During your charter, the charter base team is your first contact for any technical question, and the Boat4You team is there to help before and during your trip.
