Service Facilities on Site: A Working Shipyard

Because the marina is part of a working shipyard, boat repair, hull maintenance, and engine servicing are everyday work here. For charter guests this means that a mechanical question can usually be answered on the spot, without waiting for a technician to drive in from Zadar.

The marina lies in Sukošan bay, about 10 km south-east of Zadar, and Zadar Airport is a short drive inland. It is small compared with the large D-Marin Dalmacija marina on the same stretch of coast, which keeps arrivals and departures calm and manageable. Shore facilities cover the basics: fresh water, a small chandlery, and a quayside team who can give route advice and local tips.

Before departure, the charter partner's team walks you through the boat and its equipment. Boat4You passes your plans on in advance, so the handover can focus on the anchorages, distances, and weather that matter for your days on the water.

Fleet: Practical Day Cruisers & Runabouts

The motorboats chartered here are compact, reliable vessels: 24–32-foot day cruisers and runabouts with engines of around 150–250 hp. These boats prioritize fuel efficiency, shallow draft (ideal for approaching beaches), and practical galley/toilet arrangements—not luxury finishes.

Speeds range 16–22 knots, sufficient for Kornati day trips (40 nm round-trip in 4–5 hours). GPS and VHF radios equip every vessel; chart plotters provide navigation backup. Open seating and modest cabin shelter suit adventure-minded families; elderly passengers or comfort-focused groups might prefer larger day cruisers elsewhere.

All motorboats feature functional heads (toilets) and small galleys with basic cooking equipment—adequate for day trips, self-catering lunch, and casual overnight anchorages.

Motorboat Itineraries: Kornati, Local Islands & Hidden Coves

The Kornati National Park day trip is the classic outing from Sukošan. Leave in the early morning and you reach park anchorages such as Levrnaka or Lavsa by mid-morning, leaving five to six hours for snorkeling, swimming, and waterside dining. Return passages reach Sukošan by 6–7 PM. Boat4You recommends this itinerary for first-time Adriatic visitors.

Closer island explorations suit shorter schedules. Ugljan, just across the channel, offers fishing villages, olive groves, and small-town maritime culture. Pašman, its neighbor to the south, combines quiet anchorages with monastery history. Both islands welcome visitors in modest harbors with taverns serving grilled fish and local wine.

Multi-day motorboat charters might head west through the Ždrelac passage between Ugljan and Pašman toward Dugi Otok and Telašćica Bay, overnighting at small villages and exploring hidden coves inaccessible to larger yachts. The motorboat's shallow draft and modest size excel here.

Local Knowledge: Winds, Anchorages & Island Food

Local skippers and the charter partner's team know these waters well: which anchorages offer shelter from the afternoon maestral or a forecast bora, which island restaurants serve the best pašticada, and where to find the quietest swimming spots away from tourist crowds. Seasons, Weather & Best Times to Visit

Spring (April–May) combines warm days (18–22°C), calm mornings perfect for open-motorboat exploration, and manageable crowds. Water temperatures average 17°C. Summer (June–August) brings sunshine, warmer water (21–24°C), and reliable afternoon Maestral winds. Mid-July to mid-August sees peak boat traffic; early June and late August offer compromise.

Autumn (September–October) is ideal: stable weather, warm water (20–22°C), fewer boats, and exceptional underwater visibility. Motorboats—independent of the wind—operate safely even in light rain. Winter (November–March) is possible for experienced crews but closes most commercial operations.

Provisioning & Waterside Dining

Sukošan's small supermarket and nearby Zadar's extensive markets provide provisioning. Specialty Adriatic products include Pag island cheese (creamy, salty), local olive oil, and smoked fish. Kornat anchorages feature modest waterside taverns serving simple, authentic meals: grilled fish, calamari, mussel soups, and local Maraschino liqueur.

Advance notice allows the base crew to coordinate provisions delivery to your motorboat. Bring cash for island restaurants; payment systems remain basic in remote villages.
