Why Catamaran Charter from Olbia

Catamarans excel in Sardinian waters because their twin hulls provide exceptional stability in the 12–18 knot winds typical of the region. The wide beam offers spacious deck areas perfect for sunbathing, al fresco dining and socialising. With a shallow draft (often under 1.2 metres), catamarans reach sheltered anchorages and white-sand beaches that deeper monohulls cannot approach as closely.

Olbia is an ideal departure point for week-long itineraries that loop along the Emerald Coast, stop at Porto Cervo, explore the seven main islands of La Maddalena and return with stories of unmatched Mediterranean sailing. The catamarans on Boat4You range from comfortable 38-foot cruisers to luxury yachts of 50 feet and more, each equipped with modern navigation, safety systems and the comforts today's sailors expect.

Sailing Routes & Destinations from Olbia

The classic week-long catamaran charter explores the Maddalena Islands (roughly 30–35 nautical miles away), the granite peaks of Caprera, the famous pink beach of Budelli (protected, so admire it from the water) and the sheltered anchorages of Spargi and Santa Maria. Morning sailing times are typically 3–5 hours, leaving afternoons free for snorkelling, beach exploration and water sports.

Northern itineraries from Olbia follow the Costa Smeralda to Porto Cervo, anchor in Cala di Volpe and explore hidden bays such as Liscia di Vacca. The summer winds here are usually steady, so daily passages can balance exhilarating sailing with relaxation.

For longer charters, extended routes venture west towards the island of Asinara or south along the east coast of Sardinia. Most sailing takes place in 8–16 knots, ideal for both experienced sailors and those seeking a gentler pace. May–June and September offer steady winds and pleasant temperatures (22–26°C).

Local Sardinian Cuisine & Culture on Your Charter

Olbia's cooking centres on seafood fresh from the Tyrrhenian Sea. Bottarga (dried mullet roe) features prominently in pasta dishes, while malloreddus (Sardinian gnocchi) pair with rich meat sauces. Aboard your catamaran, simple meals become celebrations when prepared with ingredients from Olbia's markets: fresh fish such as John Dory (pesce San Pietro), local Cannonau and Vermentino wines, and pecorino sardo cheese.

The culture around Olbia blends Sardinian tradition with cosmopolitan European influence, reflecting centuries of maritime trade and modern tourism. Sailors here respect the sea, celebrate evening aperitivo time (6–7 PM) and know that the best meals are shared at sunset, anchored in a protected bay.

Wind Patterns & Sailing Conditions

Olbia and the surrounding north Sardinian coast are shaped by the Maestrale (mistral) from the north-west, which in summer typically blows at 12–18 knots and can gust to 25 knots and more in the afternoon. The Scirocco from the south-east brings warmer but less predictable conditions, while winter sees stronger systems. Skippers monitor the forecast daily and adjust routes to the conditions and the comfort of their guests.

The Strait of Bonifacio, between Sardinia and Corsica, accelerates the wind, so plan passages there carefully. Tidal ranges are minimal (well under 1 metre), which simplifies navigation. Most charter contracts do not allow night sailing; plan to be at anchor or in port before dark.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need sailing experience to charter a catamaran from Olbia?

Not if you book a skippered charter: a professional skipper handles navigation, and if you wish to sail actively, the skipper can show you how. For a bareboat charter, at least one crew member needs a recognised sailing licence and experience.

What is the best time to sail from Olbia?

May–June and September offer ideal conditions: warm air, pleasant water temperatures and steady 12–16 knot winds. July and August are busier (more crowded anchorages) and hotter. Winter offers solitude but stronger, less predictable winds.

Can I extend a charter beyond one week?

Yes. 10- and 14-day itineraries are possible. Longer charters allow deeper exploration—for example sailing across the Strait of Bonifacio to the south of Corsica or along Sardinia's east coast, if your charter contract allows it.

Are meals and provisioning included?

Provisioning is not included but can be pre-arranged through our partners' concierge. Shopping, restaurant reservations and a private chef can be arranged on request.

What happens if the weather turns severe?

Skippers monitor the weather constantly. If a strong system approaches, you shelter in protected harbours (Olbia, Porto Rotondo or Cala Gavetta in La Maddalena) and adjust the itinerary. Your safety and comfort come first.

What should I pack for a Sardinian catamaran charter?

Essentials include sun protection (high-SPF sunscreen, hat, sunglasses), lightweight clothing (cotton and linen in summer), a light jacket for evening breezes, comfortable deck shoes with non-slip soles and a reusable water bottle. Bring personal medications and a small first-aid kit. Many guests find reef shoes useful for snorkelling; some prefer to bring their own snorkelling gear.

Can I combine catamaran sailing with land-based activities?

Yes. Many longer Sardinia holidays combine a week-long catamaran charter with a stay ashore in Olbia, Portisco or on the Costa Smeralda. These combined itineraries can be coordinated for you, pairing sailing with archaeological sites, wine tastings and inland villages.
